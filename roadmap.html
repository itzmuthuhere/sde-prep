The Roadmap

An 8-month, ~34-week plan to go from fundamentals to signing an offer at a product-based company, built around weeknights of 9–11 PM and longer weekend blocks.

Core philosophy — read this before anything else

Four tracks run in parallel, not one after another:

  1. Content mastery — the concepts on each page of this site.
  2. Problem-solving volume — hundreds of coding problems, spaced and reviewed.
  3. Real projects — two things you built, deployed, and can defend for ten minutes.
  4. Job applications & interviews — applying, and sitting real interviews, for practice reps.

The mistake almost everyone makes is treating these as a sequence: "I'll apply once I'm ready." You will never feel ready. Interviewing is a separate trained skill — thinking out loud under time pressure, clarifying ambiguous prompts, recovering from a wrong turn, doing LLD on a whiteboard. You get good at it the same way you got good at loops: reps. Start practice-applying from month 4–5 — to companies you're lukewarm about — so that by the time you interview at a company you actually want, you have ten interviews of muscle memory behind you.

Projects overlap with study from month 5. Serious applications ramp up once you clear the interview-ready gate below — realistically around week 22–26, not week 34.

DSA deliberately spans months 2–4 — it's the largest single block and the highest-leverage skill for product-company loops. Everything after it is shorter because you'll already have the study habit.

The weekly template

Roughly 10 weeknight hours (5 × 2h, 9–11 PM) plus 8–12 weekend hours. The split between "grind problems" and "study concepts" is fixed so you don't have to decide each night — decision fatigue at 9 PM is real.

Mon 9–11DSA — new patternRead the pattern, do 3–4 problems on it (easy→medium). Write each solution's idea in the solved log before coding.

Sustainability rules: one full rest day is allowed and encouraged — take it, don't let it become three. If you miss a weeknight, don't "make it up" the next day; just resume. A missed week is recoverable; a burned-out month is not. Weekends are where projects and mocks live — protect them.

Phases & milestone checkpoints

Each checkpoint is a specific, testable bar — not "understand recursion" but "trace a recursion call tree on paper without running it." Tick them off as you clear them; they roll up into the dashboard on the home page.

Weeks 1–4 · Month 1

Phase 0 — Fundamentals

Programming basics through to arrays, strings, and complexity analysis. Goal: never be slowed down by syntax or I/O again — those should be automatic so all your thinking goes to the problem. Page: Phase 0 — Fundamentals.

Weeks 5–17 · Months 2–4

Phase 1 — DSA

The core of every product-company coding round. Checkpoints at weeks 8, 12, and 16 so you can catch a slipping pace early. Problem counts are cumulative and assume ~60% mediums, ~25% easy, ~15% hard.

Weeks 18–25 · Months 5–6

Phase 2 — SQL, Low-Level Design, High-Level Design

The "can you design software, not just solve puzzles" phase. Project 1 is being built alongside this — the design concepts here should show up in that project's code.

Weeks 26–30 · Month 7

Phase 3 — Full-Stack Java

Deep Core Java plus the Spring ecosystem — the stack you'll be hired to write. By now DSA is on maintenance (3–4 problems/week to stay sharp) and most weeknights go here.

Weeks 31–34 · Month 8

Phases 4 & 5 — GenAI + Electives

GenAI literacy is now a differentiator for backend roles; electives (containers, cloud, streaming) round out the résumé and unblock infrastructure questions. These are lighter — you're interviewing heavily by now.

Weeks 18–30 · parallel

Projects (overlapping)

Two projects, built on weekends. Not tutorials followed to completion — things with a real design decision, a real bug you fixed, and a deployed URL.

Definition of "interview-ready"

Use this to decide when to switch from practice-applying to seriously applying. You do not need every box to start practice interviews — start those at week ~14 regardless. But before you spend your limited referral capital and emotional energy on dream companies, you want roughly 80% of this true.

Practice-apply vs. seriously-apply

Practice-apply (from week ~14): companies you're neutral or lukewarm about. Target 2–3 applications/week. The goal is interview reps and calibration — treat every rejection as free data. Debrief each interview in your mock-interview log within 24 hours.

Seriously-apply (when the gate is ~80% met, realistically week 22–26): your target companies. 8–12 applications/week plus active referral outreach. By now you have a portfolio, a tight résumé, rehearsed stories, and a dozen interviews of experience — you're a different candidate than you were at week 14.
